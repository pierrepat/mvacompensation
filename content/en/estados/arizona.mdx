Arizona is an at-fault state with **pure comparative negligence** — one of the most favorable systems for accident victims. You can recover compensation even if you were mostly at fault. But Arizona also presents unique challenges: extreme heat, dust storms, vast rural stretches, a high uninsured driver rate, and complex jurisdictional issues when accidents happen on tribal land.

## Pure Comparative Negligence

Arizona follows pure comparative negligence (ARS Section 12-2505), identical to California's system:

- You can recover damages **even at 99% fault**
- Your recovery is reduced by your fault percentage
- No cutoff or threshold

This is more favorable than Texas (51% bar) or Florida (51% bar).

## Statute of Limitations

- **Personal injury:** **2 years** (ARS Section 12-542)
- **Property damage:** **2 years**
- **Wrongful death:** **2 years** from the date of death
- **Government claims:** Must file Notice of Claim within **180 days** (ARS Section 12-821.01)

### Tribal Land Exception

Accidents on tribal land may fall under federal or tribal court jurisdiction, with different deadlines and rules. Don't assume standard Arizona rules apply — consult a lawyer immediately.

## Average Compensation in Arizona

Arizona has **no cap** on personal injury damages in auto accident cases.

| Injury Type | Compensation Range |
|---|---|
| Soft tissue (whiplash, sprains) | $12,000 – $45,000 |
| Moderate (herniated disc, simple fracture) | $45,000 – $150,000 |
| Serious (multiple fractures, surgery) | $150,000 – $450,000 |
| Catastrophic (spinal cord, severe TBI) | $450,000 – $5,000,000+ |

## Arizona Insurance Minimums

Arizona requires **25/50/15:**

- **$25,000/$50,000** bodily injury liability
- **$15,000** property damage liability

Arizona does not require PIP or no-fault coverage. UM/UIM coverage is available but not mandatory — highly recommended given Arizona's ~12% uninsured driver rate.

## Arizona-Specific Hazards

### Dust Storms (Haboobs)
Massive dust storms can reduce visibility to near-zero. Arizona's **"Stupid Motorist Law"** (ARS Section 28-910) allows rescue cost recovery from drivers who enter flooded areas, but it doesn't bar injury claims from other drivers.

### Extreme Heat
Vehicles break down more frequently, tire blowouts increase, and pavement damage from heat causes road hazards. Heat can also mask injury symptoms.

### Monsoon Season (July–September)
Flash floods and hydroplaning are major risks during Arizona's monsoon season.

### Rural Highways
Long stretches without services. High-speed rural accidents are common and often more severe.

## Accidents on Tribal Land

Arizona has 22 tribal nations with significant land area. Accidents on tribal land create jurisdictional complexity:

- Tribal courts may have jurisdiction, or federal courts
- Sovereign immunity may apply to tribal vehicles or roads maintained by the tribe
- If the other driver is a tribal member and the accident occurred on tribal land, special rules govern

This is one of the most complex areas of accident law in Arizona. Don't try to navigate it without a lawyer.

## Steps to Take After an Accident

1. **Call 911.** Arizona requires reporting accidents with injuries, death, or $2,000+ in apparent damage.
2. **Document the scene.** Desert conditions can erase evidence quickly — photograph everything immediately.
3. **Exchange information.**
4. **Get medical attention.** Arizona heat can mask symptoms. Don't wait.
5. **File accident report** — if police don't respond, file with ADOT within 24 hours.
6. **Notify your insurance.**
7. **Contact a lawyer.**

## Immigration Status

Your immigration status does **not** prevent you from filing a personal injury claim in Arizona. Civil courts are separate from immigration enforcement. Despite Arizona's history with SB 1070, civil personal injury claims have nothing to do with immigration proceedings.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Not documenting the scene** — desert conditions degrade evidence fast.
2. **Not knowing about tribal land jurisdiction** — different rules may apply.
3. **Delaying medical treatment** — heat can mask symptoms.
4. **Not carrying UM/UIM coverage** — high uninsured rate makes this critical.
5. **Talking to the other insurer without a lawyer.**
6. **Social media posts.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I sue if I was partially at fault?**

Yes — Arizona's pure comparative negligence lets you recover even at 99% fault, reduced proportionally.

**What if the accident was on a reservation?**

Jurisdictional rules are complex. Tribal courts, federal courts, or Arizona state courts may have authority depending on the parties and location. Consult a lawyer immediately.

**Does my immigration status affect my claim?**

**No.** Arizona civil courts do not consider immigration status in personal injury cases.

**What if a dust storm caused the accident?**

Weather doesn't eliminate fault. If another driver was negligent (driving too fast in low visibility, not pulling over), they can be held liable. Document weather conditions.

**How much does an Arizona lawyer cost?**

Contingency fees, typically 33% to 40%. Nothing upfront. You only pay if they win.

**How long do cases take?**

Simple: 3 to 6 months. Moderate: 6 to 18 months. Serious/trial: 1 to 3 years.
