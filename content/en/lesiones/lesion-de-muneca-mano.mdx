A wrist injury car accident is one of the most common crash injuries that people underestimate. When you see a collision coming, your natural reflex is to brace against the steering wheel or dashboard. That bracing force — combined with the impact — fractures bones, tears ligaments, and damages tendons in your wrist and hand. Settlements for wrist and hand injuries range from $5,000 for minor sprains to over $100,000 for fractures requiring surgery. You can file a claim regardless of immigration status.

## How Car Accidents Cause Wrist and Hand Injuries

Your wrists and hands absorb tremendous force during a crash in several ways:

- **Bracing impact.** Gripping the steering wheel tightly right before impact transmits crash force directly through your wrists. This is the number one cause of wrist fractures in car accidents.
- **Airbag deployment.** Airbags deploy at 150 to 200 mph. If your hands are on the steering wheel at the 10-and-2 position, the airbag can fracture fingers, dislocate thumbs, and sprain wrists.
- **Dashboard contact.** Front-seat passengers often brace against the dashboard, leading to scaphoid fractures — the most commonly broken wrist bone.
- **Flying objects.** Loose items inside the car become projectiles during a crash and can strike and injure hands.

## Common Wrist and Hand Injuries from Crashes

- Scaphoid fracture (most common, often missed on initial X-rays)
- Distal radius fracture (Colles fracture)
- Metacarpal fractures (broken hand bones)
- Ligament tears (scapholunate ligament, TFCC)
- Tendon injuries (extensor or flexor tendons)
- Dislocated fingers or thumb
- Carpal tunnel syndrome from blunt trauma
- Crush injuries in severe collisions

## Why Insurers Undervalue Wrist Injuries

Insurance adjusters minimize wrist and hand injury claims using these tactics:

**"It's just a sprain."** Many wrist fractures — especially scaphoid fractures — do not show on initial X-rays. If the ER diagnosed you with a sprain, get a follow-up with an orthopedic specialist within 2 weeks. An MRI or CT scan can detect fractures X-rays missed.

**Ignoring long-term impact.** A wrist that heals with reduced grip strength or limited range of motion affects your earning capacity for years. The adjuster's offer rarely accounts for future limitations unless you document them with a functional capacity evaluation.

**Blaming pre-existing arthritis.** If you are over 40, the insurer may argue your wrist problems are age-related wear and tear. Your doctor must specifically note that the fracture or ligament tear was caused by acute trauma, not degenerative changes.

## How to Protect Your Wrist Injury Claim

1. **See an orthopedic specialist within 14 days.** ER doctors focus on ruling out life-threatening injuries. A wrist fracture — especially a scaphoid fracture — needs specialist evaluation and often an MRI for proper diagnosis.
2. **Follow your treatment plan completely.** If the doctor prescribes a cast for 8 weeks, wear it for 8 weeks. Removing it early or skipping follow-up appointments gives the insurer ammunition to argue your injury was not serious.
3. **Document your daily limitations.** Write down tasks you cannot do: opening jars, typing, buttoning shirts, holding your child, driving. These details directly support your [pain and suffering claim](/en/guias/car-accident-settlement).
4. **Track lost work time.** If your wrist injury kept you from working — or forced you into lighter duties with less pay — save every pay stub and get a letter from your employer documenting the impact.
5. **Do not sign anything from the insurance company.** The adjuster may send a medical records release or a quick settlement check. Both can hurt your case. Talk to a [car accident lawyer](/en/guias/spanish-speaking-car-accident-lawyer) first.

## Treatment Overview

Wrist and hand injury treatment depends on severity:

- **Minor sprains:** Splint or brace, ice, anti-inflammatory medication. Cost: $300–$1,500. Recovery: 4–8 weeks.
- **Fractures (non-surgical):** Cast or splint for 6–12 weeks, follow-up X-rays, physical therapy. Cost: $3,000–$8,000.
- **Fractures (surgical):** Open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) with plates, screws, or pins. Cost: $15,000–$40,000. Recovery: 3–6 months plus physical therapy.
- **Ligament repair surgery:** Arthroscopic or open repair. Cost: $10,000–$30,000. Recovery: 4–6 months.
- **Physical therapy:** Typically 2–3 sessions per week for 6–12 weeks post-surgery. Cost: $2,000–$6,000.

Total medical costs for a surgically repaired wrist fracture with rehab often reach $25,000 to $50,000.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does a wrist injury car accident case take to settle?**
Most wrist injury claims settle in 4 to 12 months. If surgery was needed, the process takes longer because your attorney will wait until you reach maximum medical improvement before calculating full damages. Cases that go to trial may take 18 to 24 months.

**Can I claim compensation if my wrist was already weak before the accident?**
Yes. Under the eggshell plaintiff doctrine, the at-fault driver is responsible for all harm they caused — even if your wrist was more vulnerable than average. The key is medical documentation showing that the crash caused a new injury or significantly worsened a prior condition.

**What if the ER said it was just a sprain but it still hurts weeks later?**
Get a second opinion from an orthopedic hand specialist. Scaphoid fractures are missed on initial X-rays up to 20% of the time. A delayed diagnosis does not prevent you from filing a claim, but the sooner you get proper imaging, the stronger your case.

**Do I need a lawyer for a wrist fracture claim?**
If your injury required surgery, caused you to miss work, or resulted in permanent limitations, an attorney can significantly increase your settlement. Studies show that represented claimants receive 2 to 3.5 times more than those who negotiate alone. Most car accident attorneys work on contingency — no upfront fees.
