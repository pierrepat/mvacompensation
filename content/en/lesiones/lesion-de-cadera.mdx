A hip injury from a car accident can leave you unable to walk for weeks or months. The hip is a ball-and-socket joint that bears your entire body weight — when it fractures or dislocates in a crash, recovery is painful and lengthy. Settlements for hip injuries range from $15,000 to $200,000 or more, depending on whether surgery is needed and how it affects your ability to work.

## Types of Hip Injuries from Car Accidents

| Injury Type | What's Damaged | Recovery Time | Typical Settlement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hip fracture | Femoral neck or acetabulum | 4-6 months | $50,000 – $200,000+ |
| Traumatic bursitis | Bursa (fluid sac) | 4-8 weeks | $10,000 – $25,000 |
| Hip dislocation | Femoral head leaves socket | 2-3 months | $30,000 – $80,000 |
| Acetabular fracture | Pelvic socket | 3-6 months | $40,000 – $150,000 |
| Labral tear | Cartilage ring | 4-6 months | $20,000 – $60,000 |
| Avascular necrosis | Bone death from blood loss | Chronic/surgery | $75,000 – $300,000+ |
| Sciatic nerve injury | Nerve compressed by injury | 3-12 months | $25,000 – $80,000 |

## Warning Signs After an Accident

- Intense pain in the hip, groin, or thigh when walking or standing
- Inability to bear weight on the affected leg
- Leg appears shorter or rotated outward (fracture or dislocation)
- Swelling and bruising around the hip area
- Pain radiating down the leg to the knee
- Clicking or locking sensation when moving the hip

If your leg appears shorter or rotated after a crash, go to the ER immediately. A hip dislocation is an emergency that can cause permanent damage if untreated within 6 hours.

## How Hip Injuries Happen in Crashes

**Frontal impact** — when your knee strikes the dashboard, force transmits directly through the femur into the hip joint. This is the most common cause of hip fractures and posterior dislocations in car accidents.

**T-bone impact** — the door deforms inward and strikes the hip directly, causing acetabular fractures and pelvic injuries.

**Pedestrian accidents** — vehicle impact against the hip causes severe fractures.

## Why Hip Injuries Are Expensive

Hip injuries often require surgery ($30,000-$50,000 for a hip replacement alone), months of physical therapy, and carry long-term risks of post-traumatic arthritis that may require future hip replacement.

## What Your Case May Be Worth

- **Simple fracture without surgery** — $15,000-$30,000
- **Fracture with surgical hardware** — $50,000-$100,000
- **Total hip replacement** — $100,000-$200,000+
- **Permanent disability** — $150,000-$500,000+

## FAQ

**Can I claim if I had pre-existing hip arthritis?**
Yes. The "eggshell plaintiff" doctrine means the at-fault party takes you as they find you. If the accident worsened your condition, that's compensable.

**How long do I have to file?**
Most states allow 2 years from the accident date. Don't wait — earlier action preserves evidence.

**Do I need a lawyer?**
For hip injuries, legal representation almost always helps. The amounts involved are high and insurers fight aggressively to minimize payouts.
