Parking lot accidents account for roughly 20% of all vehicle accidents according to the National Safety Council. While speeds are typically low, injuries can be serious: whiplash, head injuries, and back injuries are common. Your compensation depends on who was at fault and the severity of your injuries.

## Who's at Fault?

The general rule: **the moving vehicle has more responsibility than the stationary one.**

| Situation | Who's at Fault? |
|---|---|
| Car backs out and hits you | The reversing driver |
| Two cars back out simultaneously | Shared fault (50/50 or per circumstances) |
| Hit while parked | 100% the other driver |
| Collision in the driving lane | Normal traffic rules apply |
| Pedestrian hit in parking lot | The driver (must yield to pedestrians) |

## What to Do After a Parking Lot Accident

1. **Don't leave** — leaving is a crime (hit and run), even on private property
2. **Call police** — they do respond to parking lot incidents. The police report documents the accident officially
3. **Photograph everything** — both vehicles, positions, parking lot markings, security cameras, license plates
4. **Find security cameras** — most parking lots have them. Ask the manager to preserve footage before it's overwritten
5. **Exchange information** — name, phone, insurance, license plate
6. **See a doctor** — even if you feel fine. Whiplash and back injuries can appear hours or days later

## Compensation Ranges

| Injury Type | Typical Settlement |
|---|---|
| Mild whiplash | $5,000 – $15,000 |
| Lower back injury | $10,000 – $40,000 |
| Herniated disc | $30,000 – $80,000 |
| Head injury/concussion | $20,000 – $100,000+ |
| Knee injury from dashboard | $15,000 – $50,000 |

## When the Parking Lot Owner Is Liable

The property owner may share liability if:
- **Confusing signage** — unclear arrows, contradictory directions
- **Poor lighting** — dark areas that prevent visibility
- **Dangerous design** — lanes too narrow, blind curves, poorly designed ramps
- **Lack of maintenance** — potholes, ice, oil spills

## FAQ

**What if the other driver left?**
This is hit and run. File a police report immediately. Security cameras can identify the driver. Your uninsured motorist coverage may cover damages.

**Can I claim without a police report?**
Yes, but the report strengthens your case significantly. Without it, the insurer will question everything.
